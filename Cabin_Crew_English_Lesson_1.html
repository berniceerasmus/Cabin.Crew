✈ CABIN CREW ENGLISH

Lesson 1 — Welcome Aboard!

Getting to Know You • Diagnostic Speaking • Grammar Check • Cabin-Crew Communication • Game • Homework

Lesson Snapshot

Length45–50 minutes
FocusSpeaking, listening, grammar, vocabulary, interaction
Professional contextCabin crew / passenger communication
Teacher goalObserve natural English without making the lesson feel like a test.
Teacher rule for Lesson 1: Let them speak. Avoid correcting every mistake. Record useful examples and use the separate assessment pack after the lesson.

0–5 min — Welcome & Set the Tone

TEACHER VIEW: Welcome both students. Explain that today is about getting to know each other and practising useful English. Do not call it a placement test.
STUDENT VIEW:

“Today is about getting to know each other and seeing how we can use English for your future career in cabin crew. Don't worry about mistakes — just communicate and enjoy the activities.”

5–17 min — Getting to Know You

TEACHER VIEW: Student A interviews Student B, then swap. Ask follow-up questions when answers are short. Listen for question formation, sentence length, tense control, vocabulary and confidence.
  1. Tell me a little about yourself.
  2. Where are you from and where do you live?
  3. What do you currently do?
  4. What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
  5. What kind of music, films or TV shows do you like?
  6. Tell me something interesting about yourself.
  7. Why do you want to become cabin crew?
  8. Why are you interested in Turkish Airlines?
  9. What do you think would be the best part of being cabin crew?
  10. What do you think might be difficult about the job?

Useful follow-ups: Why? / Really? Tell me more. / Can you give me an example? / What about you?

17–25 min — Cabin-Crew Conversation

TEACHER VIEW: One student is cabin crew and one is the passenger. Do not give them model answers first. Swap roles. Increase difficulty if they cope easily.
Situation 1 — Seat: “Excuse me, I think this is my seat.” Then: “But I want to sit next to my husband.” / “Can I change seats?”
Situation 2 — Nervous passenger: “I'm really nervous. I've never flown before.”
Situation 3 — Food: “I ordered a vegetarian meal, but this isn't vegetarian.”
Situation 4 — Luggage: “Can you help me put my bag in the overhead compartment?”

25–34 min — Mini Grammar Challenge

TEACHER VIEW: Let them answer independently first. Ask for complete sentences. Notice whether they understand the meaning as well as the form.
1. Present simple: I usually ______ at 7 o'clock.
2. Present simple: I ______ English twice a week.
3. Present continuous: At the moment, I ______ English.
4. Past: Tell me three things you did yesterday.
5. Future: What are you going to do this weekend?
6. Prediction: What do you think you will be doing five years from now?
7. Experience: Have you ever travelled abroad? Where did you go?
Teacher notes: 1) wake up / get up; 2) study / learn; 3) am studying / am learning. Questions 4–7 are open answers. For #7, listen for present perfect in the first response (“Have you ever…?”) and past simple when discussing the specific trip (“I went…”). Do not require one exact vocabulary answer.

34–44 min — ✈ Cabin Crew Taboo

TEACHER VIEW: Give one student a target word. They have 30–45 seconds to describe it without using the forbidden words. Swap. Award a point for a successful guess. The aim is communication, not perfect English.
AIRPLANE
No: fly, airport, wings, travel
PASSPORT
No: travel, airport, document, country
LUGGAGE
No: bag, suitcase, clothes, airport
PASSENGER
No: person, airplane, travel, customer
TURBULENCE
No: shaking, plane, air, flying
PILOT
No: airplane, fly, cockpit, captain
SEATBELT
No: seat, safety, buckle, airplane
BOARDING PASS
No: ticket, airplane, airport, seat
Teacher observation: Can they paraphrase when they do not know a word? Do they keep talking or stop? Do they use examples, descriptions and synonyms?

44–50 min — Wrap-Up

1. What are you hoping to improve in your English?

2. What do you think is your biggest difficulty in English?

3. If you could speak English perfectly one year from now, what would you want to be able to do?

TEACHER VIEW: Thank them and explain that future lessons will combine grammar, speaking, games and realistic cabin-crew situations.

🏠 60-Minute Homework — Cabin Crew Starter Pack

Purpose: reinforce the first lesson without turning homework into a textbook exercise.

Part 1 — Vocabulary Builder (15 min)

Learn these 15 words and write one original sentence for each:

passenger
cabin crew
flight attendant
boarding pass
departure
arrival
destination
luggage
hand luggage
seatbelt
aisle
window seat
emergency exit
turbulence
boarding

Part 2 — Grammar: My Life in Three Time Zones (15 min)

Write 5 true sentences for each section.

  • Usually: things you normally do.
  • Yesterday: things you did.
  • Next weekend: things you are going to do.

Challenge: Add one question to each section and answer it.

Part 3 — 1-Minute Speaking Recording (10 min)

Make a voice recording without reading a script:

“Why do I want to become cabin crew, and what kind of cabin crew member do I want to be?”

Speak for at least 60 seconds. Try to give reasons and examples.

Part 4 — Passenger Problem (10 min)

Choose ONE:

  • A passenger is afraid of flying.
  • A passenger has lost their boarding pass.
  • A passenger wants to change seats.
  • A passenger is unhappy with their meal.

Write a short dialogue of 8–10 lines between the passenger and cabin crew.

Part 5 — Reflection (10 min)

Answer in English:

  1. What was easy for me in today's lesson?
  2. What was difficult?
  3. Which English word did I learn today?
  4. What do I want to speak about more confidently next lesson?
Teacher: If students can send the 1-minute recordings before Lesson 2, keep them. They provide useful baseline speaking evidence alongside the Lesson 1 transcript.
